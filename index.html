The Problem

Parkinson's care is fragmented. Navigating it shouldn't have to be.

More than one million Americans live with Parkinson's disease — yet in rural and underserved communities, the resources, specialists, and support systems that can help remain scattered, hard to find, and harder to use.

"This wasn't just a lack of resources. It was a system that people were actively struggling to navigate — often alone, and often without anyone showing them where to start."

The toolkit was developed in direct response to focus group conversations with patients and caregivers who shared what was missing, what was confusing, and what would actually help. The result is a practical, plain-language guide built around real needs — not assumptions.

Home Adaptations & Adaptive Equipment

A safe home environment supports independence. This section covers the tools, modifications, and strategies that help people with PD live safely and confidently at home.

  • Fall prevention: U-Step Neuro Walkers, laser canes, trekking poles, and contrasting floor tape for freezing of gait
  • Eating & dining: weighted utensils, Liftware Steady, scoop dishes, Dycem mats, and adaptive drinkware
  • Dressing tools: magnetic closures, button hooks, extra-long shoehorns, and elastic shoelaces
  • Bathroom safety: tub transfer benches, raised toilet seats with arms, and grab bars
  • Bed mobility: satin sheets, transfer handles, and step-by-step turning techniques
  • Communication: voice amplifiers, weighted pens, trackball mice, and large-key keyboards
  • Where to find equipment locally — including Centers for Independent Living
🥦
Nutrition

There is no single "Parkinson's diet" — but what you eat significantly affects how your medications work, how you feel, and how well you manage symptoms day to day.

  • The MIND Diet: a brain-healthy eating pattern combining Mediterranean and DASH principles
  • The 30/60 Rule: timing levodopa around protein intake to maximize medication effectiveness
  • Symptom-specific nutrition: managing constipation, low blood pressure, fatigue, nausea, and weight changes
  • Swallowing support: thickeners, soft food guidance, and when to request a speech therapy referral
  • A daily nutrition checklist for patients and caregivers
  • Cookbooks and the APDA recipe library for practical meal planning
🏃
Exercise

Exercise is one of the most powerful tools available to people with Parkinson's — improving balance, strength, flexibility, mood, and sleep. Aim for at least 2½ hours per week.

  • PWR!Moves: Parkinson Wellness Recovery exercises designed specifically for PD
  • LSVT BIG: intensive physical therapy for motor symptoms
  • LSVT LOUD: voice and speech exercises to maintain vocal strength
  • Other beneficial exercise types: boxing, dance, tai chi, cycling, swimming
  • Regional exercise programs and classes available in Southern/Central Illinois
  • Parkinson's Foundation exercise infographics and video resources
💊
Medications

Understanding how Parkinson's medications work — and how to time them — can make a significant difference in daily quality of life. This section translates clinical language into practical guidance.

  • How dopamine replacement medications work and why timing matters
  • Understanding ON and OFF periods — what they are and how to track them
  • Tools to remember medications: apps, pill organizers, alarms
  • Managing swallowing difficulties with medications
  • How medications relate to fall risk — and what to watch for
  • Over-the-counter supplements: what's safe, what to avoid
  • Questions to ask your doctor at your next appointment

A verified regional directory of physicians, therapists, and specialists who work with Parkinson's patients across Southern and Central Illinois — organized by specialty and location.

  • Neurologists and movement disorder specialists accessible from Carbondale, Marion, and surrounding areas
  • Physical therapists certified in PWR!Moves and LSVT BIG
  • Occupational therapists, speech-language pathologists, and mental health providers
  • Nutritionists and dietitians with PD experience (including Branz Nutrition Counseling)
  • Alternative and complementary medicine providers

Social Support, Elder Care & Transportation

Parkinson's affects the whole person — and the whole family. This section connects patients and caregivers to the community support systems that reduce isolation and ease the weight of caregiving.

  • Regional Parkinson's support groups in Southern and Central Illinois
  • Caregiver-specific resources: respite care, support groups, and stress management
  • Area Agencies on Aging: services, contacts, and eligibility

Research Foundation

Grounded in real voices. Built on real data.

This toolkit was not built from assumptions. It was designed in direct response to what patients and caregivers said — and verified against what research shows actually works.

🗣
Focus Group Research
Patients and caregivers participated in focus groups that were transcribed and coded into usable data. The barriers, needs, and gaps they identified directly shaped what the toolkit covers — and how it's written.
📚
Literature Review
Systematic review of national Parkinson's disease resources, clinical guidelines, and existing toolkits. Only trusted, evidence-based sources were used to inform recommendations and resource listings.
📍
Regional Provider Verification
All provider listings in Section 5 were verified through direct outreach — not just copied from online directories. Providers confirmed their willingness to be listed before inclusion. Accurate as of May 2026.
Theoretical Framework

The social work lens behind the design.

The toolkit isn't just a list of resources. Every design decision — what to include, how to write it, who to include it for — was shaped by core social work theory.

Systems Theory
PD doesn't exist in isolation.
The patient, family, healthcare system, community, and environment form an interconnected whole. The toolkit's seven sections mirror this — each connects to the others, because changes in one domain ripple through all the rest.
Person-in-Environment
Context changes everything.
Resources were built for where people actually live. A patient in Marion, IL has different access than someone in St. Louis. The regional provider directory was built with this lens front and center.
Empowerment Theory
People are agents, not recipients.
The toolkit is designed to build knowledge, build confidence, and build capacity. Plain language, actionable steps, and real local options that patients can actually call — not just information to absorb.
Health Literacy
Meeting people where they are.
Medical information was intentionally written at a general adult reading level. Complex concepts like ON/OFF periods, freezing of gait, and levodopa timing are explained in plain language — immediately, every time.
Impact

What this project delivers.

A toolkit is only as powerful as the change it enables. Here's what this project produces — and what it opens the door to.

A ready-to-use regional resource
A complete, verified guide across 7 care domains — ready for distribution to patients, caregivers, social workers, and healthcare providers across Southern and Central Illinois.
Reduced navigation burden
Social workers and case managers now have a vetted regional resource to place directly in clients' hands — reducing the time and effort required to piece together a care plan.
Community connections built
Provider outreach during development created new awareness among local nutrition and medical professionals. Relationship-building that continues beyond the document itself.
